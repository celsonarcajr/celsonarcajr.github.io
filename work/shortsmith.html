Shortsmith

A text-to-video web app I built: type an idea, get a finished vertical short.

Year

2026

Details

Personal project — built and maintained by me

Overview

Shortsmith is mine — I built it, I did not buy it and wire it up. It exists because short-form volume does not pay for the hours it takes. Five videos a week is five scripts, five voiceovers, five footage hunts and five assemblies, and none of that is interesting the second time you do it.

It started as a pipeline for my own channel and is now a web app anyone can use: sign up, type an idea or paste a script, choose a style, and the finished short lands in your library minutes later. Accounts, credits, a render queue and the showcase on the home page are all part of it.

Underneath, a brief goes in, a language model writes the script to a word count derived from the target duration, a narrator reads it, the footage is matched line by line from a stock library, and captions, music and the render are assembled without anyone opening a timeline.

The part I care about is the seams. Every stage is a swappable engine behind an environment flag — the script model, the voice, the footage source, the caption style, the renderer. Two providers changed underneath me while I was building it, and both times the change was a flag rather than a rewrite.

It also checks its own work. A render that reports success is not evidence: the output gets probed for duration, resolution and audio level before anything is called finished.

Result

How it works
Problem

Short-form volume costs more editing hours than it earns. A thirty-second video still needs a script, a voice, footage, captions, music and a render — do that five times a week by hand and it is a full working day gone on the least interesting part of the job.

Solution

A pipeline where every stage is a swappable engine behind an environment flag: script model, narrator, footage source, caption style, renderer. The brief goes in one end and a finished vertical video comes out of the other, and when a provider deprecates a model or changes its pricing I change a flag instead of the pipeline.

Workflow
  1. Step 01

    Brief — a topic or a finished script, plus the style, which sets the pacing, the narrator and the music.

  2. Step 02

    Script — a language model drafts to a word count derived from the target duration. A pasted script is kept verbatim; the pipeline is not allowed to improve a customer’s words.

  3. Step 03

    Narration — text to speech in the voice the style calls for, then a word-level transcript so everything downstream can line up against real timings.

  4. Step 04

    Footage match — each line is matched to stock clips by meaning, and repeats are de-duplicated by look so the same picture never comes back twice in one video.

  5. Step 05

    Assembly — shots are cut to the narration, holds are extended rather than padded with filler, and the whole thing lands on one timeline.

  6. Step 06

    Captions and music — word-timed captions burned in, a track chosen from the catalogue for that style, ducked under the voice.

  7. Step 07

    Render and verify — ffmpeg encodes the master, then the file is probed for duration, resolution and audio level. Nothing is called finished on the strength of an exit code.
